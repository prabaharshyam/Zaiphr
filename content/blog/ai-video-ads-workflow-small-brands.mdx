You know video ads work for brands like yours. The problem is making enough of them. One shoot, one edit, one ad, and three weeks later it's tired and the cost per lead has crept up.

Small teams usually stall here. There's no agency budget for weekly shoots, and the founder is already handling orders, staff and GST filings.

AI tools have made it realistic to produce a steady stream of decent short videos with a phone, a small team and a few hours a week. This post lays out a practical weekly workflow, how to structure your tests, what you must never fake, and which numbers to watch.

<KeyTakeaways>
- On Meta and YouTube, fresh creative volume usually matters more than one perfect ad.
- Build hooks from real customer language, then use AI to speed up scripts and edits.
- Test one thing at a time: hooks first, then angles.
- Never fake testimonials or results; disclose AI-generated people where it matters.
- Judge ads in order: hook rate, hold rate, CTR, then cost per result.
</KeyTakeaways>

## Why short-form video and creative volume matter

Meta (Instagram and Facebook) and YouTube now lean heavily on their own algorithms to find buyers. You give broad targeting and a budget, and the system decides who sees what. In that setup, the creative is doing much of the targeting work. A video about postpartum back pain finds a different audience from one about gym recovery, even with identical settings.

That's why common practice among performance marketers has shifted towards testing many short videos rather than polishing a single ad. Ads also wear out: the same people see the same video repeatedly and stop responding. A regular supply of new variations keeps results steadier.

Short vertical video, Reels and Shorts, is where most of this happens. It's also the cheapest format to make, which suits small brands.

## The weekly workflow

Here's a rhythm that a founder plus one marketer or freelancer can run. Adjust the days to your week.

| Day | Task | Tools | Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Mine customer language, write hooks | WhatsApp chats, reviews, ChatGPT or Claude | 15–20 hook ideas |
| Tuesday | Write scripts, plan shots | ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini | 4–6 short scripts with shot lists |
| Wednesday | Shoot phone footage, collect UGC | Phone, natural light, a lapel mic | Raw clips and creator videos |
| Thursday | Edit, add captions, make variations | CapCut, Descript, OpusClip | 6–10 ad variations |
| Friday | Add b-roll, product shots, voiceovers; launch | Runway, Photoroom, Adobe Firefly, ElevenLabs | New ads live |
| Following Monday | Review last week's numbers | Ads Manager, a simple sheet | Winners, losers, next tests |

### Monday: hooks from customer language

The best hooks are usually things customers already said. Scroll through WhatsApp enquiries, Google reviews, Instagram comments and sales call notes. Copy out exact phrases: complaints, objections, "I wish I'd known" moments.

A coaching institute might find parents repeatedly asking "Will my child get individual attention in a batch of 40?" That question is a hook. A D2C skincare brand might see "I've tried everything for my pigmentation" over and over.

Paste these phrases into ChatGPT or Claude and ask for hook variations in different styles: a question, a bold statement, a mistake, a before-and-after, a "POV" line. Ask for Hinglish or Tamil versions if that's how your customers talk.

### Tuesday: scripts with an AI assistant

Give the assistant your hook, product details, the one key benefit and your brand voice note. Ask for a 20–30 second script with a hook, a problem, the product as the answer, one proof point you can genuinely back up, and a clear call to action.

Ask it to add a simple shot list too. Then edit hard. AI scripts tend to be wordy and generic; cut every line a real person wouldn't say out loud.

### Wednesday: shoot simple phone footage and gather UGC

You don't need a studio. A recent phone, a window for light, a cheap clip-on mic and a clean background are enough. Shoot the founder explaining something, hands using the product, the clinic reception, the kitchen during lunch rush.

User-generated content (UGC) from real customers or small creators often outperforms polished brand video because it feels like a recommendation. Ask happy customers for a short selfie video, with permission to use it in ads. Our [influencer marketing](/services/influencer-marketing) team runs creator programmes if you want this at scale.

### Thursday: edit and multiply

**CapCut** is quick for vertical edits, auto-captions, text overlays and trending formats. **Descript** lets you edit video by editing the transcript, which is handy for trimming talking-head footage and removing filler words.

**OpusClip** takes a longer video, such as a webinar, a podcast or a founder's YouTube explainer, and suggests short clips with captions. One 20-minute talk can give you several ad candidates.

Make variations here: the same body with three different hooks, or the same hook with two different endings.

### Friday: AI layers, then launch

**AI voiceovers and avatars.** ElevenLabs can produce natural voiceovers, including for languages you don't speak well yourself. HeyGen and Synthesia create presenter-style videos with AI avatars, useful for explainers or translating a founder's video into other languages. Use them carefully (more below).

**B-roll.** Runway can generate short clips or extend footage when you're missing a shot, such as a monsoon street or an abstract product reveal. Keep it as supporting footage, not the main proof.

**Product shots.** Photoroom removes backgrounds and places products in clean scenes. Adobe Firefly can generate or extend backgrounds. Both are good for quick static frames and end cards.

## A simple creative testing structure

Testing ten things at once tells you nothing. Keep tests simple so you can tell what actually made the difference.

### Hook tests

Keep the body of the video the same and change only the first two or three seconds. Run three to five hooks against each other. This is the cheapest test and usually the most revealing, because if nobody watches past the opening, nothing else matters.

### Angle tests

Once you have a hook style that holds attention, test angles: different reasons to buy. A dental clinic might test "painless treatment", "evening appointments for working people" and "transparent pricing before you sit in the chair". Each is a separate video with its own hook and body.

### Keep a log

A shared sheet with the ad name, hook, angle, format, date launched and key metrics is enough. After a month or two you'll see patterns in what your customers respond to, which is worth more than any single winning ad.

<Callout type="tip" title="Name your ads properly">Use a naming pattern like angle, hook, format and date in every ad name. It makes reviewing results in Ads Manager far quicker.</Callout>

## What not to fake

AI makes it easy to produce things that look real. That's exactly where small brands get into trouble.

- **No fake testimonials.** Don't use an AI avatar, a stock actor or a scripted creator to pretend to be a real customer describing an experience they didn't have.
- **No misleading claims.** "Results in 7 days", "doctor recommended" or "India's number one" need proof you can show. If you can't back it up, don't say it.
- **No fake before-and-after.** Edited or AI-generated transformation images are misleading, and particularly risky in health, beauty and fitness.
- **Disclose AI-generated people where appropriate.** If a viewer could reasonably believe an AI avatar is a real person giving a real opinion, label it or change the format.
- **Disclose paid partnerships.** When creators are paid or gifted products, the ad should say so clearly.

The Advertising Standards Council of India (ASCI) has guidelines on misleading claims, influencer disclosures and fair advertising in general. Meta and YouTube also have their own policies, and some categories such as health and finance face stricter rules. Treat this as general guidance: check the current ASCI guidelines and platform policies, and take advice for regulated categories.

<Callout type="warning" title="Health and finance need extra care">If you sell treatments, supplements, loans or investments, get claims checked before the ad goes live. Rejected ads and complaints cost more than a review.</Callout>

## Measuring what matters

Look at the numbers in order, because each one tells you which part of the ad to fix.

1. **Hook rate.** Roughly, 3-second video views divided by impressions. Low hook rate means your opening isn't stopping the scroll. Fix the first few seconds.
2. **Hold rate.** How many viewers keep watching to a later point, such as 15 seconds or the end, compared with those who passed the hook. Low hold rate means the middle loses people. Tighten the script.
3. **Click-through rate (CTR).** The share of people who click. Low CTR with good hold usually means the offer or call to action is weak.
4. **Cost per result.** Cost per lead, per WhatsApp conversation, per purchase. This is the number that pays the bills, but it only makes sense once the earlier numbers look healthy.

Here's an illustrative example. Say an ad gets 10,000 impressions and 2,500 three-second views. That's a hook rate of 25%. If 500 people watch to 15 seconds, the hold rate from those hooked is 20%. If 120 click and you spent ₹3,000, that's a CTR of 1.2% and ₹25 per click. Run the same arithmetic on your own ads every week.

Benchmarks vary widely by category, platform and offer, so compare your ads against each other rather than against numbers from someone else's industry.

## Hook-writing checklist

Before a hook goes into an edit, run it through this list.

- [ ] Does it use words a real customer actually said?
- [ ] Is it understandable with the sound off, using on-screen text?
- [ ] Does it land within the first two or three seconds?
- [ ] Does it speak to one specific person or problem, not everyone?
- [ ] Is there a reason to keep watching: a question, a tension or a promise?
- [ ] Is every claim in it true and provable?
- [ ] Does it work in the language and mix your audience uses?
- [ ] Is the visual in the first frame interesting on its own?

## Where to start

1. This week, collect 20 customer phrases from WhatsApp, reviews and comments, and turn them into hooks.
2. Shoot one simple video on your phone and make three versions with different hooks in CapCut.
3. Set up a testing sheet and review hook rate, hold rate, CTR and cost per result every Monday.
