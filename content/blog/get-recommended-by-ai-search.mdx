A customer in Pune used to type "best dental clinic near Kothrud" into Google and scroll through ten blue links. More and more, they now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini or Google's own AI answers, and get back a short list of three or four names with reasons.

If your business isn't on that list, you may never know you lost the enquiry. And no one, including us, can promise to put you on it.

What we can do is explain what these tools seem to rely on, what plausibly helps, and how to check and track what they say about you. This is evolving best practice, not a formula, so treat it as a sensible bet that also makes your website better for humans.

## What's actually changing

People are using AI assistants as a first stop for recommendations: "Which CA firm in Chennai handles GST for small exporters?", "Good coaching for NEET in Kota with hostel?", "Affordable website developer for a restaurant?"

These tools answer in two broad ways. Some answers come from what the model learned during training. Others come from live web searches the tool runs in the background, often with sources cited. Google's AI features sit on top of its regular search index.

In both cases, the AI is trying to summarise what the web says about businesses that match the question. So the question for you becomes: when the web describes you, is it clear, consistent and believable?

<Callout type="note" title="An honest caveat">How each AI tool picks sources is not fully public and changes often. Everything below is based on how search and these tools generally work, and on what tends to help. Treat it as a set of good habits, not guarantees.</Callout>

## What plausibly helps

### 1. Describe yourself clearly on your own site

AI tools can't recommend you for something your site never says. Many small business sites are vague: "We provide quality solutions for all your needs." That gives a model nothing to work with.

Spell out:

- **Who you serve:** "Small restaurants and cloud kitchens", "Families with children aged 3–12".
- **Where:** city, areas, whether you serve all of India or ship worldwide.
- **What you offer:** each main service or product on its own page, described in plain words.
- **Prices or ranges:** "Root canal from ₹6,000", "Packages from ₹25,000". Even a range helps people and machines judge fit.
- **FAQs:** the real questions people ask on calls and WhatsApp, answered in two or three sentences each.

Write the way a customer would ask. If people say "skin clinic", don't only say "dermatological care centre".

### 2. Add structured data

Structured data (schema markup) is a standard way of labelling information on your pages so machines can read it reliably: business name, address, phone, opening hours, services, prices, FAQs, reviews.

You don't need to understand the code. Most website platforms and SEO plugins can add the common types, and any decent developer can set it up in a day or two. It won't make a weak site strong, but it removes guesswork for crawlers.

### 3. Keep your business details consistent everywhere

Your name, address, phone number, website, hours and category should match across:

- Google Business Profile
- Your website
- Directories like Justdial, IndiaMART, Practo or Zomato, depending on your sector
- Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn and YouTube bios
- Any industry associations or marketplaces you're listed on

If one place says "Sharma Dental Care, MG Road" and another says "Dr Sharma's Clinic, Mahatma Gandhi Rd" with an old number, AI tools (and Google) have to guess whether they're the same business. Make it easy for them.

### 4. Earn genuine reviews

Reviews on Google and sector platforms are some of the most visible signals about a local business. They also give AI tools the actual words customers use: "the doctor explained everything in Tamil", "delivered before Diwali as promised".

Ask every happy customer, at the right moment, with a direct link. A short WhatsApp message after delivery or a visit works well. Reply to reviews, including the critical ones, calmly and specifically.

### 5. Get mentioned by credible third parties

When independent sites mention you, it's a stronger signal than anything you say about yourself. Think local news, industry blogs, "best of" lists by real publications, partner websites, podcasts, college or association pages, and community forums.

You can't fake this, but you can work at it: speak at a local event, contribute an expert quote to a trade publication, partner with a complementary business that lists you as a partner, or publish something genuinely useful that others link to.

### 6. Answer real questions in your content

AI tools are built to answer questions, so pages that answer questions clearly are easy to use as sources. A coaching institute could publish "How much does NEET coaching cost in Jaipur, and what's included?" with honest ranges. A real-estate firm could explain "What documents do you need to buy a resale flat in Hyderabad?"

Pick the 10–20 questions you hear most and give each a clear, honest answer on your site, either on a service page or as a short article.

### 7. Make pages fast and crawlable

If a crawler can't load or read your page, nothing else matters. Basics:

- Pages load in under 3 seconds on a mid-range phone on mobile data.
- Important text is real text, not baked into images or hidden behind clicks.
- Your site isn't accidentally blocking crawlers in its settings.
- Each page has a clear title and description.
- Google Search Console shows your pages as indexed with no major errors.

## How to check what AI tools say about you today

This takes about an hour and is surprisingly revealing.

1. Write down 10 questions a real customer would ask, mixing general and specific: "best interior designer in Kochi for 2BHK", "is [your business] good?", "how much does [your service] cost in [your city]?"
2. Ask each question in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google Gemini and Microsoft Copilot, and note what Google's AI answers show in regular search.
3. Log, for each answer: are you mentioned, in what position, is the information correct, and which sources are cited.
4. Look at the cited sources. These tell you which directories, reviews and articles the tools trust for your category.
5. Fix anything wrong at the source: an old phone number on a directory, an outdated price on your site.

Repeat the same questions on the same day each month in the same sheet. Answers vary from run to run, so look for trends over a few months rather than reacting to one result.

<Callout type="tip" title="Use the citations as a to-do list">If Perplexity keeps citing a particular directory or blog for your category and you're not on it, that's your next listing or outreach target.</Callout>

## What not to do

Some shortcuts are tempting. They tend to backfire, and some are simply dishonest.

- **Fake reviews.** Buying reviews or asking staff and relatives to post as customers misleads people, breaks platform policies and can get your listing penalised or removed. Consumer protection rules in India also take a dim view of fake reviews; check current rules if you're unsure.
- **Keyword-stuffed AI spam pages.** Publishing hundreds of near-identical pages like "best plumber in [every area of Bengaluru]" generated by AI is the kind of low-value content search engines actively try to demote. It also makes your site worse for real visitors.
- **Hidden text and tricks.** Text meant only for crawlers, or instructions aimed at AI tools hidden in your pages, is risky and can damage trust in your whole site.
- **Inconsistent claims.** Saying "No. 1 in Chennai" on your site with nothing to back it up won't help, and may hurt credibility.

AI tools are, in effect, trying to work out who is genuinely good. The durable strategy is to be genuinely good and make that easy to see.

## A 30-day action plan

**Week 1: Baseline and basics**
- Run the AI check above and save the results.
- Complete every field in your Google Business Profile, including services, hours, photos and description.
- List every place your business appears online and note mismatched details.

**Week 2: Fix consistency and your own site**
- Correct name, address, phone and website on all directories and social bios.
- Rewrite your home page and main service pages to say who, where, what and price ranges.
- Ask a developer or use a plugin to add basic structured data.

**Week 3: Questions and reviews**
- Collect the 10–20 most common customer questions from WhatsApp, calls and emails, and publish clear answers.
- Start a simple review request routine: one message with a direct link after every completed job or order.
- Check Google Search Console for indexing errors and slow pages.

**Week 4: Third parties and tracking**
- Make a list of 10 credible sites that could mention you (associations, partners, local media, industry blogs) and reach out to three.
- Apply for any relevant directories the AI tools cited in week 1.
- Repeat the AI check and set a monthly reminder.
