"How much for a website?" is the question every founder asks, and the answer they usually get is "it depends", followed by a quote that's either ₹8,000 or ₹8 lakh. Neither number helps if you can't see what's inside it.

This post lays out the four common ways to get a website built in India, rough price ranges for each, what actually pushes the cost up or down, and the costs nobody mentions until after launch. You'll also get a checklist to take into your next vendor call.

<KeyTakeaways>
- Scope drives price far more than who builds it: pages, copy, design and integrations add up.
- DIY builders, freelancers, studios and custom builds each make sense at different stages.
- Budget every year for domain, hosting, maintenance and content, not just the build.
- Whatever you spend, insist on owning your domain, code and analytics.
</KeyTakeaways>

## The four ways to get a website built

The ranges below are typical market ranges we see in India. They vary widely by city, experience and scope, so treat them as rough orientation, not a rate card.

### 1. DIY builder (Wix, Framer, WordPress.com and similar)

You pick a template, drag in your content and publish. You pay a monthly or yearly plan; most builders have a free tier and paid plans that start low, so check current pricing.

The cash cost is small. The hidden cost is your time: writing every word, sourcing photos, fixing the mobile layout and learning the tool. Good for a first site, a side project or a business that just needs a clean online visiting card.

### 2. Freelancer

Roughly ₹15,000 to ₹1,50,000 for a small business site, depending on experience and pages. A good freelancer can be excellent value, especially for a five-page brochure site.

The risk is concentration. If the one person gets busy, falls ill or moves on, your updates stop. Ask who else can access the site and how handover works.

### 3. Agency or studio

Roughly ₹75,000 to ₹5,00,000 and above. You're paying for a team: strategy, copy, design, development and testing, with someone accountable for the deadline.

The range is wide because agencies vary enormously. Some resell templates with a markup; others do genuine custom work. Look at their live sites on your phone, not just their portfolio screenshots.

### 4. Custom build

Roughly ₹3,00,000 upward, often much more. This is for sites that behave like software: customer logins, booking engines, dashboards, complex catalogues or heavy integrations with your CRM or ERP.

If that sounds like you, you may actually need a web app rather than a website. Our guide to [mobile and web apps](/services/mobile-web-apps) explains the difference.

| Route | Rough range (varies widely) | Best for | Main trade-off |
|---|---|---|---|
| DIY builder | Plan fee plus your time | First site, tight budget | Your time and design limits |
| Freelancer | ₹15,000 – ₹1,50,000 | Small brochure sites | Depends on one person |
| Agency or studio | ₹75,000 – ₹5,00,000+ | Lead generation, brand-led sites | Higher upfront cost |
| Custom build | ₹3,00,000+ | Logins, bookings, complex logic | Cost and longer timelines |

## What actually drives the cost

Two quotes for "a website" can differ ten times over because they describe different things. These are the levers that move the number.

| Cost driver | Keeps it lean | Pushes it up |
|---|---|---|
| Number of pages | 1 to 5 pages | 20+ pages, service or location pages |
| Copywriting | You supply final text | Agency writes and edits all copy |
| Design | Adapted template | Custom design and illustrations |
| CMS | Static pages, rare updates | Blog, team-editable content, roles |
| E-commerce | Enquiry or WhatsApp ordering | Full catalogue, cart, Razorpay or Cashfree checkout, GST invoices |
| Integrations | Contact form to email | CRM, booking system, WhatsApp API, inventory sync |
| Multilingual | English only | English plus Hindi, Tamil or other languages |
| SEO setup | Basic titles and sitemap | Keyword research, schema, content plan |

Copywriting is the one people underestimate most. A clinic that hands over clear, final text for each service can save a real chunk of budget. A clinic that hands over nothing should expect the vendor to charge for writing, and should want them to.

E-commerce is the other big jump. Showing products with a "Order on WhatsApp" button is a small job. A store with variants, stock, shipping through Shiprocket, discount codes for a Diwali sale and GST-compliant invoices is a different project. Platforms like Shopify can make this cheaper than building from scratch.

<Callout type="tip" title="Write the scope before you ask for quotes">List your pages, who writes the copy, and every system the site must talk to. Send the same list to every vendor so you're comparing like with like.</Callout>

## The costs that keep coming every year

The build fee is a one-time cost. Running the site isn't. Budget for these from day one.

- **Domain name.** Usually a few hundred to a couple of thousand rupees a year for a .in or .com. Renewal prices can be higher than the first-year offer.
- **Hosting or builder plan.** Ranges from very low for a simple static site to meaningful monthly fees for a busy store. Ask what your plan includes and what happens if traffic spikes during a sale.
- **Maintenance.** Security updates, plugin updates, broken-form fixes, backups. Some vendors offer a monthly plan; others charge per fix. Either is fine if it's clear.
- **Content.** New blog posts, updated offers, fresh photos. A site that hasn't changed in two years quietly tells visitors you might not be around either.
- **Paid apps and plugins.** Booking tools, review widgets, premium themes. Small individually, they add up.

A simple way to think about it: add the build cost to three years of running costs, and compare vendors on that total, not just the launch price.

## Hidden costs and red flags

Most website horror stories in India aren't about price. They're about what you discover after you've paid.

<Callout type="warning" title="If you can't log in, you don't own it">If the vendor holds the only login to your domain, hosting or site admin, you're one disagreement away from losing your website. Fix this before launch, not after.</Callout>

Watch for these:

- **No source or content access.** You should be able to export your site's files or content. "We'll host it for you, don't worry" is not an answer.
- **Locked hosting.** Some vendors require you to host only with them, often at a markup, and make moving painful. Ask directly: "If I leave, what do I get and how long does it take?"
- **Domain in the vendor's name.** The domain must be registered to your business, with your email as the account owner.
- **No analytics.** If nobody sets up Google Analytics 4 and Google Search Console, you'll never know whether the site works.
- **No mobile testing.** Most of your visitors will come from phones, often on patchy data. If the vendor only shows you the desktop version, ask why.
- **Vague quotes.** "Website development: ₹60,000" with no page list, revision count or timeline is a dispute waiting to happen.

## What every site should have, at any budget

Whether you spend ₹10,000 or ₹10 lakh, these basics are non-negotiable.

- **Fast load on mobile.** Test on a mid-range Android phone on mobile data. If it takes more than a few seconds, people leave.
- **A working form and a WhatsApp button.** Many Indian customers would rather message than fill a form. Give them both, and make sure enquiries actually reach someone.
- **Analytics from day one.** Google Analytics 4 for traffic, Search Console for search visibility, and optionally Microsoft Clarity to see where people get stuck.
- **Basic SEO.** Unique page titles and descriptions, a sitemap, clean URLs, your address and phone number, and a linked Google Business Profile if you serve a local area.
- **Ownership.** Domain, hosting account, code or content, and analytics, all in your name.

## Your pre-hiring checklist

Take this into every vendor conversation. If you can't get a clear answer to any line, treat that as information.

1. Is the quote itemised by pages, features and number of design revisions?
2. Who writes the copy, and is that included?
3. Will the domain be registered in my business's name, with my login?
4. Where will the site be hosted, and can I move it if I leave?
5. Will I get admin access and a full export of my site?
6. Are Google Analytics 4 and Search Console set up and shared with me?
7. How will you test on mobile, and on which devices?
8. Are the form and WhatsApp button tested end to end before launch?
9. What are the yearly running costs, in writing?
10. What does maintenance cost after launch, and what's the response time for fixes?
11. Can I see two or three live sites you've built, and check them on my phone?
