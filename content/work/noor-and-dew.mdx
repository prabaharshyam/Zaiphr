## Overview

Noor & Dew is a fictional skincare brand from Kochi that pairs one Ayurvedic hero ingredient with one proven active in every formula. It sells almost entirely online, mostly to first-time buyers on their phones. The storefront had to explain a new brand in seconds, make choosing easy and get out of the way at checkout.

## The challenge

Skincare shoppers don't think in product types, they think in routines. A stock theme sorts by "serums" and "moisturisers" and leaves people to work out the order themselves. And in India, the checkout has to treat UPI and cash on delivery as first-class options, not afterthoughts.

## Shop by ritual

We organised the store around three rituals (morning, evening and weekly), each with three steps. Every product page says which ritual and step it belongs to. A two-minute skin quiz builds a starter ritual with sample sizes first, so trying a new brand feels low risk.

## Ingredient storytelling

Each hero ingredient gets a plain-language note on what it does and at what percentage, next to the Ayurvedic story behind it. No clinical jargon, no miracle claims. The product renders keep the range consistent: amber glass for actives, frosted jars for creams.

<Figure src="/work/noor-and-dew/range.jpg" alt="The four core products, a cream jar, two amber dropper bottles and a pump bottle, on a warm stone surface" caption="The core ritual range." />

## A checkout built for Indian habits

The mobile checkout puts UPI first, with one tap into GPay, PhonePe or Paytm, then cards, cash on delivery (with a small fee shown up front) and no-cost EMI. The address is pre-filled for returning customers and the delivery date is shown before payment, not after.

## Performance

A headless build on Next.js and Vercel, with images sized per device and the hero preloaded, targets a largest contentful paint under 1.5 seconds on a mid-range Android phone over 4G. Content lives in Sanity so the brand team can publish rituals and journal stories without a developer.

## Projected impact

Design targets for a concept, not client results: faster pages than a stock theme, fewer drop-offs between product and checkout, and a ritual structure that raises the number of products per order.
