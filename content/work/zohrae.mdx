## Overview

Zohraé makes sculptural lamps, vases and planters: each one 3D-printed in plant-based PLA, then sanded, finished and tested by hand in India. The pieces are designed to be looked at, by day as objects and by night as light. The website had to sell both of those lives, which a grid of white-background product photos cannot do.

## The challenge

A lamp's whole value is what it does to a room after dark, and that is exactly what a product photo flattens. Zohraé's price point (₹3,600 to ₹4,900 a lamp) sits well above mass-market lighting, so the site had to earn trust in the craft and make people feel the glow before they'd consider paying for it.

There was a practical brief too: eight lamps, vases, planters, framed art, personalised gifts and a wedding line, all needing to live in one store without the luxury feel turning into a catalogue.

## The idea: let people switch the lamps on

We built the experience around one interaction. Every lamp on the site has a small gold button drawn on its base. Touch it and the lamp lights up, warm and slow, the way it does at home. In the collection view a single switch turns the whole room off, so visitors can light the lamps one by one and compare them as light, not just as objects.

## Art direction

The palette is near-black, warm umber and a single honey gold, so the lamps are always the brightest thing on screen. Typography pairs a high-contrast serif for names and headlines with wide-tracked small caps for navigation, borrowed from jewellery and fashion houses. Italian section names (Lumière, L'Atelier, Matrimonio, Fatto per te) give the store a voice without a word of marketing copy.

On mobile every spotlight is recomposed rather than shrunk: the lamp sits higher, the name drops below, and the gold switch stays within thumb reach.

## Telling the craft

Luxury is believable when you can see the work. The atelier section walks through five steps, drawn by hand, printed layer by layer, finished by hand, tested in the dark and wrapped to be remembered, with the brand's own numbers alongside: around 26 hours of making in a single lamp, one pair of hands finishing each piece, and no warehouse, because everything is made to order.

The materials story is told honestly as well. The site explains that PLA is plant-based but only breaks down in industrial composting, so the pieces are designed to be kept for years rather than replaced.

## Shopping the collection

Behind the cinematic front is a working store. The Lumière collection page filters by price, sorts by what's featured and shows lamps on a warm paper background where their colours read accurately. Rooms ("Shop the room") show the lamps in real interiors with tappable hotspots, and gifting flows let customers add names and monograms or plan wedding keepsakes.

## What shipped

A fast, responsive store on Next.js and Vercel, with the interactive light switches, an atelier story, journal, gifting and wedding pages, and a checkout that takes UPI and cards. It is live at zohrae.in.
