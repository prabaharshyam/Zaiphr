## Overview

Pearlline Dental is a fictional three-chair clinic in Bengaluru. Like most clinics, it loses bookings in two places: calls that ring out while the front desk is busy with a patient, and calls that come in after closing. Pearl is a voice receptionist that picks up every one of them, books straight into the dentists' calendars and handles the follow-up on WhatsApp.

## The challenge

A missed call at a dental clinic is rarely a lost lead that calls back. It's a patient with a toothache who rings the next clinic on the map. Hiring a second receptionist doesn't fix evenings and Sundays, and voicemail is where bookings go to die. The brief: answer every call within two rings, in the patient's language, without ever pretending to give medical advice.

## How Pearl works

<Figure src="/work/pearlline-dental/flow.jpg" alt="System diagram: ring, understand intent and language, check live calendar, book, confirm on WhatsApp, remind the day before, with a clinical handoff path" caption="From first ring to filled chair, with guardrails." />

1. **Answer.** Pearl picks up on the clinic's own number, says who she is and that the call is handled by an AI assistant.
2. **Understand.** She works out what the caller wants (a new booking, a reschedule, an emergency or a question) and switches to English, Hindi or Kannada to match them.
3. **Check and book.** She reads each dentist's real chair time and treatment lengths, offers the next few free slots and writes the booking into the calendar.
4. **Confirm and remind.** A WhatsApp message with the time, map and a reschedule button goes out seconds after the call, and a reminder the day before asks the patient to confirm with a single reply.

<Insight>Anything clinical goes to a person. Pearl books, she never diagnoses.</Insight>

## The front-desk console

The team sees every call as it happens: a live transcript, the caller's intent, whether a slot is being held and a button to take over the call. The day's calendar sits alongside so staff can watch bookings land in real time.

## Guardrails we designed in

- A disclosure line at the start of every call.
- No diagnosis, no medical advice, and an instant transfer for pain, bleeding or anything clinical.
- The caller can ask for a person at any point.
- Every call logged, summarised and searchable, with consent wording reviewed for the clinic.

## Projected impact

These are design targets for a concept, not client results: no unanswered calls, most routine bookings handled without staff, and a front desk that spends its time with patients in the chair instead of on the phone.
